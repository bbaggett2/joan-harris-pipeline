## General positions (apply to any story)

**Identity, not capacity or influence.** Many will contests don't allege forgery. They accept the person signed and
argue undue influence ("he was badgered, threatened, blackmailed — he didn't mean it") or lack of capacity. "In most
of our cases, we can't help you with undue influence. That is an attorney argument." Capacity is a medical question
for doctors, especially with advanced Alzheimer's, dementia or Parkinson's. A forensic document examiner answers who
wrote it. Bart stays away from capacity and influence because the textbooks define the field as identity. Illness can
show in handwriting as shaky, illegible writing, and personality in handwriting is widely believed, but neither is
something a court accepts to settle the question.

**Credibility settles cases.** "You're not hiring me just to write a report. You're hiring me to make the other side
reconsider their motions and settle before I take the stand." Bart's name has been used in negotiations he was never
retained on (see Michael Jackson below), which he takes as proof that an examiner's credibility carries weight in
multi-million-dollar estates.

**Disappointed heirs.** "People say it's a fraud, people say it's forgery, just because they're mad they got cut out
of the will. That's most of our cases." Family members who invested ten or twenty years in someone's life, even if
they arrived after the money, want their piece of the pie. Not an uncommon story.

**Money brings out lawyers in every culture.** In any country and any script, wills and money bring out lawyers,
family members and handwriting experts arguing forgery or undue influence.

## Case notes

### Michael Jackson's 2002 will (QDE-FC-49) — memo 2026-10-02
- Randall Sullivan's biography *Untouchable* (2012) names Bart in its source notes as the handwriting expert discussed
  by Howard Mann and Katherine Jackson's attorney Perry Sanders. Bart has **no record** of that conversation: no
  report, no payment from any side, no visit to the courthouse. He has a vague memory of speaking with someone about a
  famous case and thinks it may have been this one. "I would absolutely remember if I held Michael Jackson's will in
  my hand."
- His takeaway: his name was used to negotiate in what became a losing negotiation, on a case he was never retained
  on. That is the credibility point above.
- Looking recently at the published copy of the will, Bart saw no problems; it looked highly congruent. **This is not
  an official opinion — he has not seen the original.** (Confirmed by Bart 2026-10-03.)
- The court never let the forgery claim be argued. Bart's read: "the witnesses were just too powerful, regardless of
  whether he was in New York or L.A. when he signed it."

### Larry King's handwritten will (QDE-FC-50) — memo 2026-10-02
- Bart's biggest TV appearance was sitting across from Larry King on *Larry King Live* in 2006, discussing the
  JonBenét Ramsey case (Bart's recollection; date per Bart). It was the first time they met. About 3 million viewers.
- Between takes Larry asked Bart to read his handwriting. Bart told him he was fast and smart; Larry pushed for what
  people don't know; Bart told him, with the studio gone silent, that he had a very large libido. Larry laughed, and
  the interview went well. *(Risqué — use only the gist, and only if the post's tone allows it.)*
- Bart was not called in the will dispute and believes he would have been excluded as biased because of the
  friendship from the show.
- Looking recently at the published will, he saw no reason to think it wasn't Larry's writing — Larry had very
  distinctive handwriting. **Not an official opinion.**
- "The whole world misses Larry King. I sure do. He helped my career and was super friendly and respectful to me."

### Birla will, India (QDE-FC-52) — memo 2026-10-02
- Bart first toured India in 1997 and went back about every other year for twenty years. In the 2000s the forensic
  school took a few students in India and he met two or three of the forensic experts the Indian courts favored. He
  did not test them, so he can't vouch for their skill. One wanted to partner and teach. Time zones and different
  local law ended the experiment.
- Indian cases take years to reach court, and courts keep using the experts they know, so it is a hard market to
  enter.
- The Birla dispute is mostly an undue-influence fight — see the general position above.
